# SSP Photography Learning Path

Twenty-three courses is a lot to face on day one. This is the order I actually teach in, built so each step gives you what the next one assumes you already know.

It works for any genre. The wildlife modules are here because seeing a concept applied in the field is what makes it stick, not because you have to shoot wildlife.

## The main path

Work through it in order. Some steps are a whole course, some are a few modules, and a few send you into another course and back again. That is deliberate.

### 1. Camera and Lens Buying Guide

**Before you begin, optional**

Only if you are still choosing a camera or lens. If you already have your gear, skip straight to step 2.

- What to actually look for in a body and a lens, and what to ignore.
- How to read an MTF chart, so you can judge a lens on sharpness and contrast rather than marketing.

Open the course →

### 6. Understanding Focusing

**Basics & Advanced Photography, module 6**

The key module. Auto focus modes and focus points, and how to choose the right combination for the composition you want rather than the one the camera gives you.

Open the course →

### 7. Separate focusing from the shutter

**Back Button Focusing, full course**

Take this short course right after module 6, while focusing is still fresh. It is the single change that fixes the most photographs: move focus off the shutter button and onto a button at the back, so you decide when the camera focuses and when it does not.

- Why the default setup keeps refocusing on the wrong thing at the wrong moment
- How to set it up on Canon entry level, mid range and high end bodies
- How to set it up on Nikon bodies
- Where back button focusing helps, and the few situations where it gets in the way

Shooting mirrorless? The idea works exactly the same way. The button names differ, so check your manual for the AF-ON button and apply the same logic.

Open the course →
