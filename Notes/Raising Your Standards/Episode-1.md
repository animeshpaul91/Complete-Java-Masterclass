## What is a Standard for?

Raising a Standard means deciding what quality deserves your allegiance, and giving that decision authority over your conduct. It only holds (or is durable) when you can:
* Recognize the quality.
* Say why you want it after examining it.
* Produce it.
* Return to it after you break it (or after there is a temporary pause to it).

These 4 condition are the foundations of your standard. The purpose of having a standard is to live a life where your conduct reflects your values.

It is important to recognize the quality you value. You do not pursue a standard because others have it. Your standards are a reflection of your behavior (or conduct).


## A Goal is a name for something you want to reach. 

## A Standard is a discrimination made binding: It controls how you behave, how you work, what you accept, what you refuse.

## A System is what stops you renegotiating the standard everyday.

### A Goal gives direction; A Standard protects quality; A System removes the daily argument.


### Where to raise your standards? Factors that govern the dependency stack of standards
1. You must very carefully and very deliberately choose where to demand excellence from yourself. Where you bear and want to uphold your identity.
2. You must separate your responsibility for conduct from your attachment for results.


### Where to NOT raise your standards?
You have a finite budget of willpower, attention and self-monitoring. Raising your standards everywhere will cause you to raise them NOWHERE.
How do you deal with it? **Asymmetry is the art.**
1. You need to aim to have ferociously (non-negotiably) high standards in two or three domains (at most). These are areas which should survive the disappearance of public recognition. The desire should come from within.   
2. You need to aim to have adequate standards everywhere else on purpose.

Chosen mediocrity is strategy. Unchosen mediocrity is decay. Adequacy never covers: honesty, respect for others, basic care, a promise someone relies on you for.
One of the most important standard that you can raise is the standard for what DESERVES your efforts and not what DOES NOT deserve your efforts.
